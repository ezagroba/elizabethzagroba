To shift a recent conversation away from the negative, Maaret Pyhäjärvi asked me and Alex Schladebeck to tell her about a colleague we enjoyed working with.

I recounted a story of a recent experience on my team. I'd drafted a list of team and product metrics we could start collecting, but I wasn't sure how to motivate or encourage people to care about these numbers. I presented the list and my conundrum to my product owner. He suggested talking to a developer on our team.

The developer came up with an idea I would have never thought of: he suggested presenting one metric per sprint at the sprint review. People wouldn't be overwhelmed by a dashboard that would be hard to interpret or effect the outcome of. Instead, we'd give them one number, one action to take, one habit to build, one concept to wrap their head around, and two weeks to think about it. 

I wasn't sure how people would react, but I knew it was worth a try. 

A week or so later, the day before the sprint review, the developer did something wild. He remembered our conversation. He reached out to me in a chat with an idea of which metric we should choose as the first metric of the sprint. He had a script leftover that could collect it effectively, and put the information on a slide. He even presented the first one at the sprint review. 

There was a reaction from the crowd! People were interested in the metric, what it meant for behaviors we wanted to change, habits we wanted to build, and settings we could change on our repositories to make certain tasks automatic. 

The pattern repeated in a similar way the following sprints: he remembered that this was a task he shared with me, and chatted me about what we should collect this sprint. We shared in identifying the next metric, presenting at the sprint review, and following up on questions from the crowd at the previous review. 

---

In short, my colleague:

- made my idea better
- helped me make it happen
- remembered to follow-up
- communicated asynchronously
- shared the credit

The actual work of showing the number of open branches, the number of open pull requests, or whatever metric we choose next was not the important part of this for me. It was the [trust](https://elizabethzagroba.com/posts/2017/2017-08-06_doubt-builds-trust/) I have in this colleague. It was the interest and critical thinking it prompted in the department. It's believing that I work with colleagues interested in collaborating, whose ideas make mine shine. 

I was sad to learn this week that this colleague is transferring to another department to be closer to the code on a smaller team. With his approach, I know he'll be effective and build trust wherever he goes.

Who do you work with that inspires you? How did you find them?
